The two audiences

Every file in packages/editor/src styles one of two things: the editor you use, or the website your customer publishes. Before you delete or restyle anything, decide which. This is the sheet to check.

Audience 1 — you

Editor chrome

  • Sidebar, rail, inspector, topbar, footer, modals
  • Owned by chrome-ui/ + flowbite + tw:
  • Tokens: --bk-* (generated from Figma)
  • This is the drain target. Convert it, delete its CSS.
Audience 2 — your customer

Published site output

  • Canvas contents, exported HTML, the live site on Vercel
  • Owned by blocks/, templates/, engine/
  • Tokens: --buildrick-design-* (the customer picks these)
  • Never a drain target. Deleting it breaks live sites.

Total excluded from css_lines: 1,438. The ratchet carries this list explicitly in scripts/check-styling-ratchet.mjs, one reason string per row, so an exception is distinguishable from a leak.

How to tell, in one command

# Does this class ever reach a customer's page?
grep -rhoE 'class="[^"]*your-class' src/blocks src/templates src/engine

# 33 buildrick-* class names are emitted into customer HTML today.
# blocks/Components/Slider.tsx:277 is the clearest example:
#   '<div class="buildrick-slider" data-buildrick-type="slider">'
The trap A dead-CSS scanner reads Canvas.css as 100% dead, because the engine writes those class names at runtime and they are not in any .tsx. Purging on that signal alone deletes the styling of every site your customers have already published — and they will not find out from a test, they will find out from their client.
