Validate the wedge before you build it.

The design is done and codex-clean. This is the one thing the design can't prove: do real agencies actually feel this pain? Run this before writing a line of build code.

The bet under everything: "Agencies feel real pain keeping many client sites on-brand from one place — enough to pay for it or switch tools." Every downstream move rides on this: the P0 build, the editor theme.* wiring that doesn't exist yet (LR-0017). No agency has confirmed it. This test either funds the build or kills it — both outcomes save you.

1 · Who to talk to

3–5 real web/digital agencies running ≥3 client sites. People who would actually buy — not friends (they lie to be nice). Reach: cold DM, agency Slack/Discord, a local meetup, your network's network. You need strangers with the problem, not fans of you.

2 · The method — diagnose, don't pitch

You are a doctor, not a salesman. The moment you describe Buildrik, they start being polite — and polite data is worthless. Three rules from The Mom Test (Rob Fitzpatrick):

1. Their life, not your idea. Ask about how they work today. The second you mention your solution, the answers bend to flatter it.
2. Past specifics, not future opinions. "Would you use X?" = worthless (everyone says yes). "Show me the last time this happened" = gold (it's a fact, not a guess).
3. Talk less, listen more. Your job is to learn, not to convince.
Compliments are not data. "Cool idea," "sounds useful," "I'd definitely try it" = a polite no. Real signal is pain + what they've already spent on it.

3 · The five questions

All past-tense, all specific. Notice there is zero "would you use Buildrik" — that's a pitch, not a question.

  1. Abhi kitne client sites manage karte ho? Last waala kaam dikhao jahan brand (logo / color / font) badalna pada.
  2. Jab ek change saare pages / saare sites pe lagana tha — last baar wo kaise hua? Kitna time laga, kitne tools, kaun karta hai?
  3. Cross-client consistency pe pichle mahine kya toota? Kisi client ne pakda ya complain kiya?
  4. Iske liye abhi kya use karte ho, aur kitna pay karte ho? (Webflow / Figma / manual copy-paste?)
  5. Last naya tool kab adopt kiya — kis dard ne switch karaya?

4 · Show the design — last

Only after you've mined their real pain, show design-handoff.html (the clickable prototype) + the ★ agency-wedge journey in wireflows.html. Then shut up and watch. Their reaction to a real flow tells you far more than their opinion on a pitch. Do they lean in and say "wait, can it do my 5 clients at once?" — or just nod politely?

5 · Read the signal

▲ PULL — go
  • unprompted current pain
  • already spends money / hours on it
  • "when can I get this?"
  • offers intros to other agencies
  • tries to use it right now
▽ NO-PULL — kill
  • "cool idea"
  • hypothetical enthusiasm, no past pain
  • "sounds useful"
  • compliments, no budget
  • "I'd maybe try it someday"

6 · The gate

≥ 3 of 5 agencies show real spent-money-or-time pain on this exact problem → fund the P0 build (start the theme.* editor wiring). < 3 → the wedge isn't the wedge: pick the other differentiator (the agency review-loop — CEO-lens called it the sharper retention pain, LR-0017) or stop. Either result is a win — you learned before building, not after a wasted quarter.

Logistics

20-minute calls. Record with consent. One notes doc, one row per agency: their pain (specific), what they spend today, pull/no-pull. Don't sell — diagnose. You already have everything you need to run this: the handoff (217 screens) + the wedge journey. Figma can wait — it makes this prettier, not truer.

Primary source: The Mom Test — Rob Fitzpatrick (how to talk to customers so the data is real). Method taught in Lesson 25.
