A model agency interview — annotated.

The demand-gate call, run end-to-end against a (fake) agency owner — Sara, who runs a 6-person web agency. Re-read this before each real call. The annotations show why each move works. Real pull only comes from real agencies — this just teaches the moves.

The scene. Sara runs a small web agency (~12 active client sites on Webflow). She's busy, gets pitched constantly, and will be polite the moment you describe your product. Your job: learn her real pain without ever pitching — until the very end.

The call

You

"No pitch — I just want to learn. Sara, tell me about the last time a client asked for a brand change across their whole site — logo, colors, or font. What happened?"

Why: Didn't touch your idea. "Last time" = a past specific. Asked about her life, not your tool.

Sara

"Ugh, just last month. A restaurant client rebranded — new logo, new colors. Their site was on Webflow, 14 pages. I had to open every page myself."

You

"14 pages manually... how long did that take, and who does that work?"

Why: Grabbed the number ("14 pages") and dug the cost — time, who. Asked for a fact, not a compliment.

Sara

"My junior did it, a whole day. And honestly... an old logo got left in one footer. The client caught it three days later. Awkward."

Why this is gold: An unprompted failure story — real pain she volunteered once the question was specific enough.

You

"What did that footer-logo slip do to the client relationship?"

Why: Dug the consequence — pain that compounds beyond the first answer.

Sara

"Client was a bit annoyed — 'you guys are usually careful.' Small thing, but a scratch on trust. Now I double-check every rebrand myself, which eats more time."

You

"For multi-page changes like that, what do you use today — any tool, or manual?"

Why: Current solution + its gap = the exact slot your wedge fills. Still no pitch.

Sara

"Webflow plus a shared Figma where we keep brand assets. But Figma-to-Webflow is all by hand. Copy, paste, every page. No 'change once, apply everywhere.'"

You

"What do you all pay for this today — Webflow, Figma, anything else?"

Sara

"Webflow per-site, Figma seats, and... honestly the most expensive thing is my junior's time. Every rebrand is basically one-to-two days of labor. We do 2-3 a month."

Why: Money quantified — tool fees + the real cost, labor-days × 2-3/month. This is the "spent money/time" signal the gate needs.

⚠ Here's where a new founder slips. Sara just named the pain — you'll want to blurt "That's exactly what we're building — change once, apply everywhere!" Don't. The second you pitch, Sara goes polite — "oh nice, sounds useful" (= zero signal). One more question, then show the design last.

You

"When did the agency last adopt a new tool, and what pushed the switch?"

Why: Finds her buying-trigger — when she actually switches — so you learn how to sell later.

Sara

"Moved to Webflow from WordPress last year — maintenance was a nightmare for clients. We switched when two clients had hacked-site complaints. We move when the pain becomes client-facing."

Now the design — last

You

"Can I show you one thing — tell me if it touches your restaurant-rebrand case?" (share the handoff + the ★ wedge journey)

Sara

(leans in) "Wait — this changes one master brand and pushes it to every client site? One click? ...That saves me a day on every rebrand. When can I get this?"

"When can I get this?" = PULL. A reaction to a real flow, not an opinion on a pitch. Because you hid the idea, this reaction is real.

Verdict — Sara = ▲ PULL
  • unprompted pain ✓ (the footer error, she volunteered it)
  • spends money / time ✓ (2-3 labor-days a month)
  • "when can I get this?" ✓
  • current workaround has a clear gap ✓ (Figma → Webflow, manual)

1 of 5 done. Sara counts. You need ≥3 of 5 to fund the build (see the gate).

The five moves you just saw

  1. Hid the idea till the end → every answer stayed honest.
  2. "Last time" / "how long" / "who" — past + numbers, never "would you."
  3. Dug the consequence (trust, extra QA), not just the first answer.
  4. Spotted the pitch-trap — felt the urge, stopped.
  5. Showed the design LAST → got a real reaction, not politeness.
This was a demo — Sara is fake. Real pull only comes from real agencies. Send the outreach, run 5 calls, follow this same arc. After your first real call, bring the notes back — we'll read it together: pull, or just politeness?
