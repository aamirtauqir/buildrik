The 10 heuristics, severity, and the audit loop

Your vocabulary for turning "this feels weird" into a named, prioritised, fixable finding. Source of truth: Nielsen Norman Group.

The 10 usability heuristics

Every screen is judged against these. When something feels off, it almost always violates one. The Buildrik column is where you've already felt it.

#HeuristicPlain meaningFelt in Buildrik as…
1Visibility of system statusAlways tell the user what's happening, fast."No expected result" — I click and nothing confirms it worked.
2Match the real worldSpeak the user's language, not internal jargon."Typography / div.h2" instead of "Text" — engineer words, not client words.
3User control & freedomClear exits, undo, cancel — no traps.No obvious way out of a mode; actions feel one-way.
4Consistency & standardsSame word / action = same meaning everywhere."Features have no relation" — each one looks and behaves differently.
5Error preventionStop the mistake before it happens.An edit silently changes 12 things you didn't mean to.
6Recognition rather than recallShow the options; don't make me remember them."User doesn't understand what a feature does" — I must remember, not see.
7Flexibility & efficiencyShortcuts for experts, simple path for novices.One density for everyone; power users and clients get the same wall.
8Aesthetic & minimalist designCut anything irrelevant; it drowns the relevant."Density issue / AI slop" — too much on screen, no hierarchy.
9Recognise, diagnose, recover from errorsPlain-language errors that say how to fix it.A red toast with a code, no next step.
10Help & documentationBest if not needed; if needed, in-context.I open a panel and have to guess what it's for.

Severity — what to fix first

A finding without a severity is a to-do you'll never prioritise. Rate every one 0–4. Severity = frequency × impact × persistence (how often, how much it hurts, does it keep hurting).

RatingMeaning
0Not actually a problem.
1Cosmetic — fix only if you have spare time.
2Minor — low priority.
3Major — important, high priority.
4Catastrophe — must fix before shipping.

Source: NN/G — Severity Ratings for Usability Problems.

The audit loop (5 steps)

  1. Pick one screen + one task. e.g. "Editor inspector — change a heading's colour." Not the whole app.
  2. Walk the task slowly. One observation per line. Don't fix yet — just notice every place you hesitate, get surprised, or can't tell what happened.
  3. Name the heuristic. For each observation, cite which of the 10 it breaks. If you can't name one, it may not be a real problem.
  4. Rate severity 0–4. Be honest. Most "feels weird" lands at 2–3.
  5. Sort by severity, fix the 4s and 3s first. Re-walk the task to confirm the fix.

Method source: NN/G — How to Conduct a Heuristic Evaluation.
