Reference · States checklist · companion to Principle #13

A surface isn't done until every state is designed.

The happy path — full of content, nothing loading, nothing wrong — is about 10% of the work. The other 90% is what users actually hit: empty, loading, error, offline, too-long. Run any control, panel, or screen through this list. The four marked must are the ones amateurs skip and pros never do.

Interaction states (any clickable thing)

DefaultAt rest, nothing happening. The "resting" look you usually design first.
HoverPointer over it — signals "this is clickable".
FocusReached by keyboard (Tab). A visible ring — required for accessibility.
Active / pressedThe moment of clicking — brief feedback that the press registered.
DisabledCan't be used right now — and ideally a hint why.

Data states (anything that shows content)

Empty mustNo content yet. The most important teaching moment: say what this is, why it's empty, and what to do next.
Loading mustFetching / working. Skeleton or spinner — never a frozen blank.
Full / has contentThe normal populated view.
Edge: too-long / too-many mustA 60-char title, 200 pages. Does it truncate cleanly, wrap, or break the layout?

Outcome states (after an action)

SuccessIt worked — a calm confirmation ("Saved", "Published").
Error / failed mustIt broke. Say what happened, in plain words, and how to recover — never a dead end.
WarningAllowed but risky — confirm before the irreversible.

App / mode states (Buildrik-specific)

Saving… / SavedIn-progress vs done. (See principle #2 — one status, one truth.)
Offline / ReconnectingConnection dropped → "changes queued", not "Save failed".
Publishing… / Published / FailedThe publish lifecycle — the hero action needs every step shown.
Selected / locked / hiddenEditor element states on the canvas.
Preview-as-client / collab-activeMode changes — gated until the feature is ready (principle #3).

The completeness test (3 questions)

Before calling any surface "done", answer these out loud:

  1. Empty: what does a brand-new user see before any content exists?
  2. Wrong: what happens when the action fails or the connection drops?
  3. Too much: what happens at 10× the content — long names, many items?

If you can't answer all three, the prototype isn't complete — it's just the happy path. A "complete prototype" of a surface is that surface drawn in every state above.
