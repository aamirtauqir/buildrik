Lesson 10 · ~7 minutes

To make a product feel finished — fast — fix the spine and hide the rest.

Users called the product "shit" and said "make another version." You asked the right next question: how do I fix the existing one, fast? The answer isn't speed at fixing everything — it's choosing what to fix and what to hide. A product feels finished when its one core journey works perfectly and nothing broken is visible. That's reachable in weeks, not a rewrite in months.

Three moves, in order
1. Hide what's half-baked (instant credibility).
2. Tell the user what's happening on every action (kills "feels broken").
3. Walk the one core journey until it's flawless (the spine).

Why "fix everything" is the trap

Faced with 50 problems, the instinct is to fix all 50. That's "boiling the ocean" — slow, and nothing ships, so the product stays broken the whole time. The opposite works: a product where the spine is perfect and the half-baked is hidden reads as finished, even with ten features gated off. Webflow didn't ship every feature on day one — it shipped a flawless core and hid the rest behind "coming soon… elsewhere." Judgment concentrates on the few paths people actually walk.

Move 1 — Hide before you fix

The fastest credibility you can buy: make nothing broken visible. A broken or "coming soon" feature on screen does more damage than a missing one — it trains users that the product can't be trusted. Gating it off is usually a one-line flag, not a rebuild. Your collaboration feature is the textbook case: it's demo-only, so hide it until it's real (constitution #3). The "broken features" complaint can largely vanish in a day.

Move 2 — Tell the user what happened, every time

"I never know if it errored or succeeded" was your users' core complaint, and it's the single biggest reason a working product feels broken. The fix is one consistent feedback pattern — every action shows in-progress → success, or error→recover — applied to every action. This is repetition, not invention, which is exactly where AI is fast. One pattern, many places.

Move 3 — Walk the one core journey until it's flawless

Pick the single journey your product exists for — for Buildrik, "build and publish a client's site." Walk it end to end as a real user. The moment you hit anything silent, confusing, or broken, fix it, then walk again from the top. Repeat until you reach the end with zero friction. This "walk-and-fix loop" is how your own V1 shipped. Perfect the spine; defer the limbs.

The reframe that saves you months: your product isn't shit — it's half-wired. The expensive half (the backend) exists. What's missing is the cheap half: showing states and finishing the wiring. A rewrite throws away the working half and repeats the same mistakes. These three moves finish the cheap half in the right order. Full plan: the recovery roadmap.
