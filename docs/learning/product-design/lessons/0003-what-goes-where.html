Lesson 3 · ~6 minutes

What goes where — and how loud.

You looked at a topbar and asked two designer questions on your own: "should the whole Northwind / Acme / acme-main / Home path be here?" and "should Publish be in the topbar, or somewhere else?" Both questions have the same answer underneath — two small rules that decide placement and prominence for every control in a product.

The two rules
1. Prominence = how often it changes/is used × how much it matters.
2. The single loudest button = the product's purpose.

Rule 1 — Prominence = frequency × importance

Every control competes for the user's eye. You decide who wins by asking two things about it: how often does the user touch or change it, and how much does it matter when they do? Multiply. High × high → loud and central. Low × low → tucked away or one click deep.

Rule 2 — The loudest button is the product's purpose

A screen usually has exactly one primary button — filled, coloured, the thing your eye hits first. That button should name the single reason the product exists. To find it, finish this sentence: "this tool exists so the user can ___."

A website builder exists so the user can publish a site. → The hero button is Publish, top-right.
In the first mockup I made Ask AI the blue hero. Wrong — AI is a helper, not the purpose. You caught it. Publish is now the hero; Ask AI shrank to a quiet icon.

This is why "should Publish be in the topbar?" answers itself: the most important action lives where it's most reachable and most prominent. Burying it in a menu would hide the one thing the whole tool is for.

Carry this forward

Next time any control feels like it's in the wrong place or the wrong size, don't argue taste — score it: how often × how much. And for any screen, point at the one loudest button and check it names the product's purpose. If it doesn't, that's your finding (Lesson 1), already half-fixed.
