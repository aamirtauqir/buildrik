Move the boxes. Keep the wires. Then check the current still flows.

You asked the three questions every redesign-to-build has to answer: how do we implement it, how do we do it without breaking the working logic, and how do we know it's actually right. There's one idea behind the first two, and a ladder behind the third.

The build, in two ideas
1. A redesign re-lays-out, it never re-wires — because presentation and logic are separate.
2. "100% right" isn't a feeling — it's a ladder of checks, climbed in order.

Idea 1 — presentation is separate from logic

Your topbar already has all its behaviour: undo runs the engine's undo, Publish kicks off a deploy, the save status comes from the real save state. That behaviour is the logic, and it lives outside the topbar. The topbar itself is "dumb" — it's handed the state and the callbacks as props, and all it does is draw them and call them when clicked.

That separation is what makes a redesign safe. When you regroup the topbar into three zones, you move buttons around and restyle them — but each button keeps calling the exact same handler it always did. You change where it is and how it looks, never what it does or where its data comes from.

Move the boxes, keep the wires. A change that touches only layout and style cannot break the business logic — because it never touches the logic. That's not luck; it's the payoff of building dumb presentation components in the first place.

Idea 2 — climb the verification ladder

You can't prove software is perfect. You build confidence one rung at a time, each rung catching a different mistake:

RungCatches
1 · Types (tsc)A wire plugged into the wrong-shaped socket
2 · TestsA known behaviour that stopped working
3 · Walk every stateA state you forgot to draw (empty, error, offline…)
4 · Click every actionA wire that quietly came loose in the move
5 · AcceptanceAnything that doesn't match what we agreed to build

Done = all five pass. Skipping the bottom rungs is how a change that looks finished ships a dead Publish button. The full ladder is your new Definition of Done.
