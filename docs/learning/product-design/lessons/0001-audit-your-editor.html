Lesson 1 · ~8 minutes

Audit first. Here's the one skill it needs.

You asked: "do we audit first?" Yes. But an audit isn't an opinion ("this feels weird") — it's a list of named, rated problems. This lesson gives you the naming muscle, on your own editor.

The win by the end: you can take one real annoyance in Buildrik and turn it into a finding like — "Inspector says Typography / div.h2 → violates Match the real world → severity 3 → fix: rename to 'Text'." That sentence is fixable. "It feels weird" is not.

Why "feels weird" fails you

When a screen is off, your brain feels it but can't act on it. The feeling has to become a cause. The fastest way to find the cause is to check the screen against 10 known rules — the usability heuristics. Almost every "weird" maps to one of them. That's the whole trick: stop describing the symptom, name the violated rule.

Your message already listed the symptoms. Here's the translation:

You saidIt's actually…
"density issue, too much"Aesthetic & minimalist design
"features have no relation"Consistency & standards
"user doesn't get what a feature does"Recognition rather than recall
"action has no expected result"Visibility of system status

A finding has 5 parts

This is the format you'll write every audit line in. Memorise the shape, not the words:

Screen + task · what you observed · heuristic broken · severity 0–4 · the fix

Worked example from the old Buildrik inspector (before today's redesign):

Editor inspector, selecting a heading · the panel showed div / h2 and a "Typography" section · Match the real world (CSS jargon, not a client's words) · 3 · rename to "this heading" + "Text". (This is exactly the fix we shipped today — now you can see *why* it was a 3, not a taste call.)

Your turn — name the violation

Pick the heuristic each real Buildrik moment breaks. Click an answer; you'll get the why immediately. Recall it from the table above — don't peek.

A user clicks "Publish" and nothing on screen changes for several seconds. Which rule breaks?

The inspector shows 40 controls at once with no hierarchy. Which rule breaks?

Every panel names and lays out the same idea differently. Which rule breaks?

The assignment

Open the editor (localhost:3000/edit/:id). Pick one task — say "change a button's colour." Walk it slowly and write 3 findings in the 5-part format above. Don't fix anything yet. Bring them back to me and we'll rate + prioritise them together — that's Lesson 2.
