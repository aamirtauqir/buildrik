Lesson 7 · ~7 minutes

The happy path is 10% of the design.

You spotted the two things that separate a real designer from a hobbyist: a design isn't one picture, it's a set of states — and a dozen principles can't cover everything, because rules grow with the situation. Both are exactly right. Here's how pros handle each.

Two truths you just named
1. A surface is done only when every state is designed — empty, loading, error, edge — not just the full, idle, happy one.
2. Principles stay few and general; situation-specific rules grow below them as patterns.

Truth 1 — design the whole set of states

When you drew the topbar at "Saved", you drew one frame of a film. The same bar also has to exist as Saving…, Offline — queued, Reconnecting, Publishing…, Published ✓, and Publish failed — plus hover, focus, disabled. A "complete prototype" is the surface drawn in all of them.

Amateurs design the state that looks good in a portfolio: full of content, nothing wrong. Users mostly meet the other states — the empty list on day one, the spinner on slow wifi, the red error when it breaks. Those are where products win or lose trust.

The four that get skipped, and never should: Empty Loading Error Too-much (edge). Your new states checklist lists them all, and the 3-question completeness test: what does empty look like? what does wrong look like? what does 10× content look like? Can't answer all three → it's not done.

Truth 2 — principles stay few; patterns grow

You said: "twelve won't be enough — more rules will come, depending on the situation." Correct — but the new rules don't pile onto the principle list. They live one level down. A healthy design system has four layers:

LayerWhatHow many
PrinciplesGeneral truths ("subtract before you add")Few — kept small on purpose
PatternsReusable answers to recurring situations ("how we do an empty state", "how we confirm a delete")Grow freely
ComponentsThe real buttons, cards, inputsMany
TokensColour, spacing, type valuesA handful

So the "situation-dependent" rules your instinct predicted are real — they're patterns. Keeping principles few is what stops the constitution from becoming the same cluttered mess we're fixing in the UI. Add a pattern when a situation recurs; touch a principle only rarely.
