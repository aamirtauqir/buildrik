Reverse-wireframe: let the backend tell you what's missing.

You asked: can we wireframe the app's real current functionality — everything the backend already does — and is that a job for the plan-review skills? Yes to the first; no to the second, and the difference is the whole lesson. This is the direct tool for your own diagnosis: the product isn't broken, it's half-wired.

Forward vs reverse wireframing
A forward wireframe draws the product you wish existed. A reverse wireframe draws the product that already exists in the backend. For a half-wired app, the second one is the map you actually need — it shows the gap between what the code can do and what the screen lets a user do.

Why the backend is the source of truth

Taste can be wrong. A competitor's screen can mislead. The backend can't — it's a precise, honest list of every capability the product really has. Buildrik's backend right now:

25 tRPC routers · 50+ services · 58 Prisma models. Forms, CMS collections, reviews, comments, notifications, publishing, share-links, redirects, analytics, billing — all built. The question is never "does the capability exist?" It's "does a user ever get to reach it?"

Three verdicts — and only two make wireframes

GhostBackend works, frontend shows nothing. A user can't reach it at all. → Wireframe it.
HalfFrontend shows it, but missing states or integration (no empty/error, or not wired to real data). → Wireframe the missing states (L7).
WiredFully exposed, all states, real data. → Nothing to draw. Leave it.

The Ghost + Half rows are your wireframe worklist. You no longer guess what to draw — the gap tells you. And you order it by user pain (L2), not by what annoys you.
