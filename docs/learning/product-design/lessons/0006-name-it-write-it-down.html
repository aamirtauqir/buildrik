You built a method. Here's its name — and how to make it scale.

You asked three questions at once: what do we call this thinking, can it be better, and can it design the whole editor? They have one answer. What you've been doing has a name, the upgrade is to write it down, and writing it down is exactly what lets it cover every surface.

The name
A small set of stated rules = design principles. Applying them consistently everywhere = a design system / design language. Deciding by rule instead of by taste = principle-driven design.

Taste vs principle — the whole difference

"AI-designed" and "properly designed" aren't about talent. The difference is where the decision comes from. Taste says "this looks better." A principle says "Publish is the hero because the loudest button is the product's purpose." One you can't defend or repeat; the other you can hand to anyone and get the same answer. A product feels coherent when every decision came from the same short list of rules.

The upgrade: write the rules into one page

Through five lessons, your principles were scattered — one here, one there. Scattered rules can't keep a product consistent, because no one can hold them all at once (not you, not a builder, not a future teammate). The upgrade is to collect them into a single page everyone shares:

Your twelve rules now live in one place: the Buildrik Design Constitution. Every future decision cites a number from it. Every builder ("vibcoder") gets handed it. When two people disagree, they point at a rule, not at an opinion. That single page is the "shared skeleton" your editor was missing — at the level of thinking, not just code.

Why this scales to the whole editor

This is the part that answers "can we design all of it this way?" — yes, and it's the only way that produces consistency. A design system isn't a pile of screens; it's a few rules applied many times. The same twelve principles that fixed the topbar will fix the rail, the inspector, the panels — and because they're the same rules, the surfaces will finally match. Consistency isn't something you add at the end; it's what falls out automatically when every surface obeys one constitution.
